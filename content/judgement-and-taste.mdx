My philosophy on design and code work: design was never about hand-drawing
squares, the same way software development was never about writing the code.
That was never why someone paid you, or trusted you to do the thing you do.

The artifact is just the residue. What you're actually hired for is
judgement — which square, why there, what to cut, when it's done — and
taste: knowing what good feels like before you can explain it. Two people
with the same tools and the same brief ship wildly different things, and the
difference was never typing speed.

AI just made this impossible to ignore. Machines draw the squares now, and
they write the code. If your value was the production step, that's gone. If
it was the judgement wrapped around it, you've never had more leverage.

It always was. We just used to bill for the squares.
