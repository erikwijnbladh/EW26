A control panel can only do what's drawn on it. Everything it can do is
visible, and everything it can't do is invisible in the same way — as nothing.
Four sliders, and that's the language.

A text box is the other way round. Nothing on it says what it can do, which
also means nothing on it says what it can't. A panel's ceiling is its floor. A
box has a high ceiling and no floor at all.

I built a small thing to look at it. Same square, two ways in.

The panel has every dimension the square has, so there's no state the box can
reach that it can't. And the thing behind the box is a page of regular
expressions, not a model — worth saying, since a demo that implies
intelligence it doesn't have is showing you something else.

A few things I noticed.

Typing `heavier` moves three controls at once. The useful part isn't that it's
quicker than dragging three sliders, it's that nobody had to decide "heavier"
was a thing. Every control on a panel is a decision someone made before anyone
used the product — this gets a slider, this gets buried, this doesn't ship. A
text box doesn't carry that list, so it never had to be right about it.

Then ask it for something it doesn't know. `flip it`, or `add a shadow`.
Nothing happens, and there was no edge you could have felt beforehand. You find
the limits by hitting them, one at a time, and each miss tells you almost
nothing about where the next one is. A slider never does that to you — you
can't ask it for something it doesn't have.

Smaller version of the same problem: `lighter`. Lighter in colour, or lighter
in weight? Mine picks colour. A model would also pick one, more gracefully,
and you still wouldn't know which reading you were getting until after you
pressed enter.

The chips under the box are the same admission. I put them there and then wrote
a paragraph against them, which is roughly what everyone else has done —
suggestion cards, prompt starters, slash commands, a row of buttons for the
four things people actually do. That's a control panel set down next to the
box, because the box didn't come with a way in.

So it depends on how big the space is. When what someone might want is too
large to list — writing, code, images — a box is the honest option, and the
list was never going to work. When the list is short enough to draw, draw it.
Nobody wants to type "turn the volume down a bit".

The part I keep chewing on is that this isn't really a model problem. A better
model raises what's possible and does nothing about sitting in front of an
empty box with no idea what to say to it. Every fix I've seen for that is a
panel in disguise, including mine.
