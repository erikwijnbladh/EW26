Software is mostly handoffs. One part of a system produces something, another
part has to work out what to do with it, and at every one of those boundaries
there's the same choice: the producer says what it means, or the consumer
guesses.

Guessing is cheaper to build. Look at the shape of the thing, infer the intent,
branch on it. No agreement to negotiate, no vocabulary to keep, no meeting.

## what guessing costs

- **every new case moves downstream.** the producer adds a variant, the
  consumer needs a release.
- **the rule lives nowhere.** it's distributed across conditionals, and no
  single place says what anything means.
- **correct and lucky are indistinguishable.** a wrong inference produces
  output shaped exactly like a right one.

The third is the real cost. Because a bad guess looks like a good one from the
inside, the system can't tell you it's guessing badly. It just quietly is,
until something downstream is wrong for reasons nobody can locate.

## the alternative is old and boring

Discriminated unions, MIME types, status codes. The producer declares, and the
consumer renders the declaration rather than reconstructing it.

I built an audit tool once where the backend's response shape varied per
account. Instead of the frontend inspecting payloads to decide what to draw,
the backend put classifiers on the object headers — `has_nested_rows` and
friends — and the frontend never read the data at all. Absence declared
something too.

It isn't free. A missing classifier meant a row silently didn't render, and the
vocabulary was an agreement rather than a type, so it only held while both
sides kept it.

But the shape of the trade is general: **whoever knows the answer should say
it.** Leaving it to be reconstructed doesn't remove the work. It moves it to
whoever is worst placed to do it, and hides it from whoever decides what things
cost.

Which is where I keep landing on model output. You read it and infer your way
through — what's solid, what's invented, what needs checking before you use it.
A confident wrong sentence is shaped exactly like a confident right one.

We solved this for machines decades ago by making producers declare. Nothing
here declares anything, and most of the effort is going into better output —
which raises the value of the guess without removing it.
