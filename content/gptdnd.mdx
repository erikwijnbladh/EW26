gptdnd turns a brief into a complete 5e-compatible campaign and renders it to a
printable book, a Foundry VTT module, a Roll20 bundle and Universal VTT maps.
Everything is generated into one canonical graph with strict schemas and
deterministic seeds; every output format is a renderer over that graph.

The premise it's built on is that the product is an adventure production
system, not a chatbot.

## I don't know how to play D&D

That's not a disclaimer, it's the reason the thing is shaped the way it is.

I couldn't tell you whether a generated campaign is any good. I have no taste
in the domain — no instinct for whether an encounter is balanced, whether a
mystery is solvable, whether a session is paced right. So instead of trusting
my own judgement of the output, I had to encode what "good" means and make the
system prove it every run:

- encounters carry their arithmetic — XP thresholds, the multiplier ladder,
  action-economy warnings, measured versus target difficulty — printed into
  the book rather than hidden
- stat blocks are CR-normalised, with XP and proficiency derived rather than
  guessed, and outlier detection on AC, HP and damage
- a continuity critic reads the finished text back
- conclusions supported by fewer than three clues are flagged as a defect
- safety lines and veils are compiled into every prompt *and* re-scanned
  against the finished text
- every run ends with a quality report

None of that would exist if I could just read the output and know.

## Making a guess show its work

The interesting problem was never generation. It was that the thing produces
confident output I have no way to verify, which is the same problem as any
interface sitting on top of a model.

The answer I keep arriving at is that the system has to show its work — not
its reasoning, its arithmetic. "This encounter is hard" is a claim. "This
encounter is 4,200 XP against a 3,800 threshold, ×1.5 for four creatures" is
something you can argue with.

## What it costs

Cost control is built in rather than bolted on. The style guide, rights
posture, safety policy and brief live in `instructions` and are identical
across every call in a run, so prompt caching serves them; only the per-node
payload changes. Cached and uncached input are metered separately and the
running total is emitted as a pipeline event — `41 model calls, $2.18, cache
hit rate 63%`. `store` defaults to false, because campaign text is user
content.

It also runs with no API key at all: `pnpm demo` produces the full export
bundle from deterministic fixtures.
