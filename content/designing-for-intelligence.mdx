This is a thought I keep coming back to but haven't really explored yet:
ambiguous systems are hard to design for.

Almost everything I know about building interfaces assumes the system is
predictable. A button does one thing, and the design work is making that one
thing clear. Trust comes almost for free — the interface keeps its promises
because it can't do anything else.

The systems I find myself working with now don't behave like that. They
interpret. You give them something open-ended, they guess at what you meant,
and they act on the guess — sometimes impressively, sometimes confidently
wrong. I don't fully know what it means to design for that yet.

Two things feel especially hard. Intention: how does an interface help
someone express what they actually mean, when the input is a blank text box
instead of a labeled button? And trust: how do you earn it when you can't
promise the outcome — when the honest answer to "what will happen if I do
this?" is "probably what you want"?

Maybe part of it is designing the system's uncertainty. When it should
hesitate, ask instead of act, show its work. I'm not sure. Mostly I'm
writing this down so I stop circling it and start working on it.
