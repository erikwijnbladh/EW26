Every AI product ends up as a text box. It reads as laziness — as though
nobody got round to designing the thing — and I don't think it is. A box is
the only interface that doesn't have to know in advance what you'll want from
it.

That's the trade underneath it. A panel's ceiling is its floor. A box has a
high ceiling and no floor at all.

Mine is a page of regular expressions, not a model, which is worth saying —
a demo that implies intelligence it doesn't have is showing you something else.

A panel is a list of predictions. Someone decided, before anyone showed up,
which things were worth a control and which weren't, and that list became the
product. Nobody had to decide `heavier` was a thing. That's where the range
comes from — not from the box doing more, from it never having to guess.

What it gives up is any sense of where the edges are. You find them by hitting
them, one at a time, and a miss tells you nothing about where the next one is.
That part scales badly. Ask a model for something past what it can actually do
and you don't get a wall, you get a plausible answer — the same failure, except
now it's invisible.

The words aren't stable either. `lighter` — colour, or weight? Mine picks
colour. A model picks one too, more gracefully, and either way you don't find
out which until after.

The chips under the box are the tell. I put them there and then wrote a
paragraph against them, which is what everyone else has done as well:
suggestion cards, prompt starters, slash commands, a row of buttons for the
four things people actually do. Each one is a control panel set back down
beside the box, because the box shipped without a way in.

So it comes down to how big the space is. Too large to list — writing, code,
images, most of what people point a model at — and the box is the honest
answer, because the menu was never going to work. Small enough to draw, and
you should draw it. Nobody wants to type "turn the volume down a bit".

What I keep chewing on is that none of this is a model problem. A better model
raises the ceiling and does nothing at all about someone sitting in front of an
empty box with no idea what to say to it. Every fix for that so far has been a
panel in disguise, including mine.
